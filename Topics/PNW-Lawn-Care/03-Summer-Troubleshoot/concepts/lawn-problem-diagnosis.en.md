# Lawn Problem Diagnosis Guide — PNW Zone 8b

**Created**: 2026-04-05
**Applies to**: Tehaleh, WA / Cool-season grass / 0.3 acres

---

## Quick Diagnosis Flow

```
Brown/yellow patch observed
      ↓
Is it uniform?
  ├─ Uniform → Nutrient deficiency or drought (overall)
  └─ Non-uniform → Disease or sprinkler issue or pests
        ↓
Is it circular?
  ├─ Circular → Fungal disease (Brown Patch / Dollar Spot)
  └─ Striped/irregular → Uneven coverage
```

## 10 Key Problem Diagnosis Table

| # | Symptom | Likely Cause | Diagnosis Method | Solution | Treatment Timing |
|---|---------|-------------|-----------------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Circular brown patch (5–30 cm) | Brown Patch fungus | Check for pink/white mycelium at patch border | Fungicide + stop evening watering | Summer, hot & humid |
| 2 | Coin-sized circular brown spots | Dollar Spot fungus | Check for hourglass-shaped lesion on blade | Nitrogen fertilizer + fungicide | Spring–Summer |
| 3 | Irregular brown stripes | Sprinkler coverage uneven | Run Manual Run to check coverage area | Adjust head direction/height | Any time |
| 4 | Overall uniform yellowing | Nitrogen deficiency | Soil test (N level) | Apply nitrogen fertilizer | Spring/Fall |
| 5 | Persistent yellowing in specific zone | Overwatering or poor drainage | Check for waterlogged soil in that zone | Reduce Run Time, improve drainage | Summer |
| 6 | Thin grass density, bare soil | Excessive thatch or low density | Measure thatch layer (over 1.25 cm = problem) | Dethatching + Overseeding | **Fall** (after August) |
| 7 | Moss spreading | Shade, poor drainage, acidic soil | Measure pH (6.0 or below = acidic) | Apply lime, improve drainage, prune trees | Spring/Fall |
| 8 | White powder on grass blades | Powdery Mildew | Try rubbing off the white powder on blade | Improve shade conditions, fungicide | Spring–early summer |
| 9 | Irregular brown patches + grass lifting | Grubs (beetle larvae) | Flip soil beneath patch to check for larvae | Grub-specific insecticide | Late summer–Fall |
| 10 | Rapid weed proliferation | Low grass density | Identify weed type | Post-emergent herbicide per weed type | Spring–Summer |

## Fungal Disease In-Depth Guide

### Brown Patch
- **Conditions**: Temperature 25°C+, high humidity, evening watering
- **Appearance**: Circular brown patch, dark brown border ring
- **Prevention**: Water in early morning, avoid excess nitrogen fertilizer
- **Treatment**: Scotts DiseaseEx Lawn Fungicide

### Dollar Spot
- **Conditions**: Nitrogen deficiency, alternating dry and hot conditions
- **Appearance**: Coin-sized (5–8 cm) circular brown spots, hourglass pattern on blades
- **Prevention**: Adequate nitrogen fertilization, avoid excessive drought
- **Treatment**: Nitrogen fertilizer + fungicide

## Thatch Management

**What Is Thatch?** Organic matter layer of grass stems, roots, and blades (between soil surface and grass)

| Thatch Thickness | Condition | Action |
|-----------------|-----------|--------|
| Under 0.5 cm | Normal (moisture and insulation benefit) | Maintain |
| 0.5–1.25 cm | Caution zone | Monitor |
| **Over 1.25 cm** | **Problem** | **Dethatching required** |

**How to Check My Lawn**: Use a small trowel to see a cross-section → measure the brown layer thickness

**Dethatching Timing**: Fall (September–October) — after pre-emergent effect expires, during active grass growth

## Dormancy vs. Death Identification

| | Appearance | Test | Recoverable? |
|--|-----------|------|-------------|
| **Dormancy** | Uniform brown | Pull test shows resistance | ✅ 2–4 weeks after watering resumes |
| **Death** | Uneven brown, patches | Comes out easily, no roots | ❌ Overseeding required |

**PNW Summer**: Dormancy starts after 2+ weeks without watering → normal occurrence, do not panic

## Sprinkler Coverage Problem Diagnosis

If you see stripes or irregular brown areas:
1. Run Manual Run
2. Visually confirm coverage range of each station while it runs
3. Find clogged or misdirected heads
4. Clean or re-adjust heads

**Common Causes**:
- Grass has grown over and is blocking the head
- Head filter clogged (debris)
- Head has sunk lower, reducing spray angle
