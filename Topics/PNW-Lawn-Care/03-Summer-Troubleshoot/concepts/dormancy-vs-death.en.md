# Dormancy vs. Death — The Truth About Brown Grass

**Created**: 2026-04-05
**Applies to**: PNW Zone 8b / Cool-season grass

---

## Key Distinction

> **Brown grass ≠ Dead grass**

During PNW summers (July–August), when watering is stopped for 2+ weeks, Cool-season grass naturally enters **Dormancy** — a survival mechanism.

---

## Comparison Table

| | Dormancy | Death |
|--|----------|-------|
| **Appearance** | Uniform tan/brown | Uneven brown, patch-like |
| **Pull Test** | Resistance (roots alive) | Comes out easily, no roots |
| **Cause** | High heat + dry stress (normal) | Disease, pests, chemical damage, extreme drought |
| **Recovery?** | ✅ Recovers 2–4 weeks after resuming watering | ❌ Cannot recover, Overseeding required |
| **Extent** | Widespread, uniform | Localized, patchy |

---

## Field Identification Tests

### 1. Pull Test (Most Reliable)
1. Grab a handful of brown grass and pull firmly
2. If there is resistance and roots hold → **Dormancy** ✅
3. If it comes out easily with no roots → **Death** ❌

### 2. Check Color Pattern
- Uniformly brown overall → likely Dormancy
- Circular/irregular patch pattern → suspect disease or death

### 3. Center vs. Edge Color
- Dormancy: Center and edges of patch are similar in color
- Fungal disease: Patch edges are darker or have a distinct border ring

---

## Dormancy Management Strategies

### When Allowing Dormancy
- When the goal is water conservation in summer
- Condition: Maintain minimum watering of 0.25–0.5 inches/week to keep roots alive
- Natural recovery 2–4 weeks after fall rains resume

### When Preventing Dormancy (Keeping Green)
- Requires consistent watering of 1–1.5 inches/week
- Seasonal Adjust 140–150% + increase Water Days
- Early morning watering essential to minimize evaporation

### Caution: If Watering Restarts Mid-Dormancy
- Partial recovery followed by another drought means 2x the stress
- **Decision Principle**: If allowing dormancy, commit to it; if keeping green, be consistent
