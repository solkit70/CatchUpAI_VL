# Expert Mowing Guide

## Core Principle: Frequently, Moderately, Sharp

> "The grass is growing like it's at the gym. You have to work it out. You have to stress it. That's what gets it to get green."

Mowing is not just about appearance — it is **core lawn health management**. Regular cutting prevents upward growth and pushes grass to grow sideways and downward → building thickness and stronger roots.

---

## Mowing Height by Season

| Season | Recommended Height | Notes |
|--------|--------------------|-------|
| Spring (Mar–May) | 2.5–3 inches | Supports new growth after winter |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | 3–4 inches | Root shade & moisture retention |
| Fall (Sep–Oct) | 2.5–3 inches | Preparing for cold weather |
| Winter (Nov–Feb) | **2 inches** | Reduces Snow Mold and matting risk |

> "In spring, we like to keep our grass 2.5 to 3 inches. We really like 2.5 inches. We found that to be kind of our sweet spot."

**Mower height setting reference:**
- 5-level Honda/Ego models: middle setting is approximately 2.5 inches
- 7-level models: 3rd–4th notch

---

## The 1/3 Rule (Most Important)

> "You do not want to cut more than a third of the blade."

- Only the top 1/3 of a grass blade is green → the lower portion is yellow
- Cutting more than 1/3 at once → exposes yellow portion → **scalping (yellowing)**
- This is why bi-weekly mowing doesn't work: grass grows too long and forces you to cut too much at once
- Grass grows exponentially in proportion to its length → the sooner you cut, the slower it grows

---

## Mowing Frequency

- **Recommended**: At least once a week; 2–3 times per week is ideal
- **Avoid bi-weekly**: Grass grows too long, making the 1/3 rule impossible to follow
- Rainy spring: Mow whenever weather permits

---

## Blade Maintenance

> "Keep your blade sharp. If you ever noticed on your grass... it's actually split and torn instead of cut."

- Dull blade: **tears** grass instead of cutting → white tips, entry point for fungus
- **Sharpen or replace blade at least once per year in spring**
- Professional services: approximately $7 per blade
- Equipment: Can be sharpened directly with a grinder

---

## Mowing Pattern Rotation

> "Every three weeks, they'll try and rotate that on their lawns. They'll start one way, the next week they'll go 90 degrees."

- Mowing the same direction every time → grass leans one direction (Burn In Pattern)
- **Recommended rotation**: Rotate 0° → 90° → 45° every 3 weeks
- To achieve a stadium stripe pattern, intentionally maintain the same direction (but this is not ideal for lawn health)

---

## Bagging vs. Mulching

### Neighbors Lawn Services Recommends: **Bagging**

> "Bagging, my biggest thing is I want to prevent thatch."

- **Mulching**: Leaves cut grass clippings in place → returns nutrients, but risks thatch buildup
- **Bagging**: Prevents thatch, improves air circulation
- Ideal thatch depth: 1/8–1/4 inch (protects roots) → anything more is a problem

**Compromise**: Bag normally; if thatch is insufficient, mulch for a few weeks

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Never mow wet grass**: Creates clumps, clogs mower, spreads disease
- **Never over-cut**: Cutting more than 1/3 causes yellowing
- **Never let it grow bi-weekly or longer**: Causes irreversible stress
- **Never leave clippings on wet grass overnight**: Sitting wet clippings invite fungus

---

## Tehaleh Application Points

- More frequent mowing → thicker lawn (no space for weeds like POA to take hold)
- Mow low before winter → prevents moss and fungus (moss is a serious problem in Tehaleh)
- If lawn turns too yellow after mowing: try **increasing mowing frequency first** before reaching for nitrogen fertilizer
