# Aeration, Dethatching & Overseeding Guide

## The Correct Order

> "Our process, the way that we do it, is actually backwards to this slide. We'll go in and dethatch first."

```
Dethatching → Aeration → Overseeding
```

Why this order matters: Removing thatch first prevents aeration holes from getting clogged, and allows seeds to make direct contact with the soil.

---

## 1. Dethatching

### What is Thatch?
- Layer of dead grass stems, roots, and organic matter accumulated on the surface
- **Healthy amount**: 1/8–1/4 inch → protects roots, retains moisture
- **Problem level**: 1 inch or more → blocks air, moisture, and fertilizer; suffocates grass

### Signs of Thatch Problems
- Lawn feels spongy underfoot
- Fertilizer has no effect
- Water pools on the surface (not absorbed)
- Blades sit noticeably high even at 2.5-inch mowing setting

### Recommended Dethatching Frequency
- **With normal maintenance**: Once every 4–6 years
- **If needed annually**: Review your mowing, watering, and nitrogen management

> "You shouldn't be dethatching every year. If you do your lawn renovation and all that, and you go right, it should be a once every five years process."

### DIY Method
- Home Depot rental: approximately $100/day
- The large volume of debris generated must be taken to a dump (sod with mesh cannot go in regular recycling)
- Applying moss killer before dethatching increases effectiveness (weakened moss pulls out more easily)

---

## 2. Aeration

> "If you do nothing to your grass every year, I suggest aerating it at least once a year."

### How It Works
- Power machine pulls out cylindrical soil plugs (~1 cm diameter)
- The holes created allow air, water, and nutrients to penetrate
- Relieves compaction caused by Tehaleh's glacial till soil

### After Aeration
- **Plug disposal options**:
  1. Run mower over them to break up and spread on surface (easiest)
  2. Collect and fill holes with compost (more effective)
- **Topdressing recommended**: Put light compost into aeration holes → replenish organic matter

### DIY Method
- Home Depot rental: approximately $100/day

### Best Timing
> "The best time to do aeration and dethatching is in the fall."
- **Optimal**: August–September (grass actively growing, less stress)
- **Reality**: 90% of homeowners do it in spring (to address winter moss)

---

## 3. Overseeding

> "If you go to most yards, there's a lot of red thread. Once you do pull out those sample diseases and stuff, make sure you overseed it."

### Why It's Needed
- Grass lifespan is 7–10 years → needs regular replenishment
- Repair damaged or diseased areas
- Maintain Kentucky Bluegrass ratio

### Seed Selection
- **Tehaleh recommended**: Same Country Green Turf Farms blend
- **Alternative**: Lowe's 50-pound bag (Kentucky Bluegrass + Rye + Fescue mix)
- Neighbors' on-site overseeding: Rye-focused (fast germination, 2 weeks)

### Pre-Overseeding Preparation
- Mow grass **below 2 inches** (so seeds can reach the soil surface)
- Complete Dethatching + Aeration first

### Seeding Rate
> "I see at three or four pounds per thousand square feet."
- For a typical front yard: 3–4 pounds is sufficient

### Watering During Germination (Special Rules)
> "I'm watering three to five times a day. I'm sorry for your water bill this month, but it's just part of the process."

| Stage | Watering Frequency | Notes |
|-------|-------------------|-------|
| Right after seeding → germination | 2–5 times/day | Surface must not dry for more than 2 hours (slide says 2x/day; instructor personally does 3–5x/day) |
| After germination | Gradually reduce | Until new grass reaches 2+ inches |
| After first mow | Return to normal schedule | |

- Duration: approximately 4–6 weeks until germination complete
- Seeds that dry out → die, must re-seed

### Overseeding After Pre-emergent
- **Important**: Wait **8–10 weeks** after applying pre-emergent before overseeding
- Pre-emergent suppresses germination of all seeds → including lawn seeds

### Best Timing
> "Again, best to do in August or September."
- **Optimal**: August–September
  - Right temperature, allows 2–3 mowings after germination
  - Roots form over winter → results visible in spring
- **Spring overseeding downside**: New seeds immediately exposed to summer drought and heat

> "I tell them that you're not going to see much results in the fall. You're going to see my work really working in the spring."
