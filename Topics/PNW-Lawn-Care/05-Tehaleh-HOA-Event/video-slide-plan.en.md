### Slide 07 `[PHOTO+BULLET]`
**Visual**: 🎨 SVG — Soil cross-section infographic: top layer diagram showing "2" turf → 2" topsoil → glacial till bedrock." Structure is communicated more clearly than a photo.
**Title**: The Glacial Legacy
**Subtitle**: Problems created by Glacial Till soil
**Badge**: Soil

**Items**:
- Only **2 inches** of topsoil — hard bedrock starts just below
- Builders installed sod on minimal topsoil → insufficient root space
- Rainwater blocked by bedrock pools near surface → overwatering, moss growth
- Drainage improvement: French Drain / Catch Basin installation recommended

**Narration Script**:
> There's a reason lawn care is tough in Tehaleh. When the glaciers retreated, they took most of the topsoil with them. As a result, there are only about 5 centimeters — roughly 2 inches — of topsoil beneath your lawn before you hit solid glacial till. Builders laid down the bare minimum of soil before installing sod. Rainwater can't penetrate the bedrock, so it pools near the surface, causing waterlogging and moss problems.

### Slide 08 `[BULLET]`
**Visual**: 🟩 REMOTION — Green background, bullet layout
**Title**: Soil Management Strategy
**Subtitle**: How to beat poor soil conditions
**Badge**: Soil Management

**Items**:
- **Soil test** every 2–3 years (target pH 6.0–7.0), approx. $100
- If pH is low (acidic) → apply Lime
- **Topdressing**: apply 1/4" of compost after aeration
- 6 fertilizer applications per year to compensate for shallow topsoil nutrient deficiency
- Chronic drainage issues → professional French Drain installation

**Narration Script**:
> Despite the poor soil conditions, there are ways to overcome them. Start by getting a soil test every 2–3 years. For around $100, you'll get a pH reading, nutrient levels, and a prescription for what your lawn needs. The ideal range for Tehaleh lawns is pH 6.0 to 7.0. If the soil is too acidic, apply lime to correct it. After aerating, spread a thin layer of compost — a practice called topdressing — to replenish the organic matter your shallow topsoil lacks.

### Slide 10 `[TABLE]`
**Visual**: 🎨 SVG — Side-by-side contrast infographic: ❌ red zone (daily shallow watering, surface roots) vs ✅ green zone (2–3x/week deep watering, deep roots). Root depth comparison diagram is more effective than text.
**Title**: Right Way vs Wrong Way to Water
**Subtitle**: This difference determines the strength of your lawn
**Badge**: Watering

**Table**:
| | ❌ Wrong Method | ✅ Right Method |
|--|----------------|----------------|
| Frequency | A little every day | Deep, 2–3 times per week |
| Result | Roots stay near surface | Roots grow deep |
| Downside | Dies immediately in drought | Builds drought resistance |
| Time of day | Evening (❌) | 4–8 AM (✅) |
| Target amount | — | 1 inch per week cumulative |

**Narration Script**:
> When it comes to watering, how you water matters more than how much. Many people water a little every day — but this keeps roots near the surface. When summer heat arrives, those shallow roots scorch quickly. Water deeply 2–3 times per week instead, and roots will chase moisture down into the soil, building far better drought resistance. Always water in the early morning, between 4 and 8 AM, before the sun rises. Watering in the evening leaves your lawn wet overnight, inviting fungal disease.

### Slide 13 `[BULLET]`
**Visual**: 🟩 REMOTION — Green background, bullet layout
**Title**: The Golden Rules of Mowing
**Subtitle**: Small habits make a big difference
**Badge**: Mowing Principles

**Items**:
- **1/3 Rule**: Never cut more than 1/3 of the grass blade at once → causes yellowing
- **Blade maintenance**: Sharpen at least once a year — dull blades tear grass
- **Rotate direction**: Change mowing direction 90° every 3 weeks — never repeat the same pattern
- **Bagging recommended**: Prevents thatch buildup and PNW moss problems
- **No wet grass**: Causes clumping, clogs mower, spreads disease

**Narration Script**:
> There are golden rules to follow when mowing. The most important is the one-third rule: never cut more than one-third of the blade height in a single session. Cutting more than that causes yellowing. Also, sharpen your mower blade at least once a year in spring. A dull blade tears rather than cuts, and torn edges invite fungal infection. And never mow on a rainy day or while morning dew is still on the grass.

**Title**: Understanding N-P-K

**3 Insights**:
- 🌿 **N — Nitrogen**: Leaf growth + green color / Spring & summer focus / e.g., 30-0-4
- 🌱 **P — Phosphorus**: Root development / ⚠️ Tehaleh soil already has enough → avoid high-phosphorus fertilizers

**Narration Script**:
> Understanding the three numbers on a fertilizer bag — N-P-K — helps you choose the right product. Nitrogen is the first number; it drives leaf growth and green color, and is the focus in spring and summer. Phosphorus aids root development, but Tehaleh's Country Green blend soil already has sufficient phosphorus, so you should avoid high-phosphorus fertilizers. Potassium strengthens cell walls and protects against frost damage — it's the key ingredient in fall and winter fertilizers.

### Slide 18 `[BULLET]`
**Visual**: 🎨 SVG — 3-step process flow: ① Dethatching → ② Aeration → ③ Overseeding with connecting arrows + icons and descriptions per step. A flowchart communicates the sequence more intuitively than text bullets.
**Title**: The Right Order
**Subtitle**: Dethatching → Aeration → Overseeding
**Badge**: Annual Renovation

**Items**:
- **① Dethatching**: Remove built-up organic material from the surface — must be done first for subsequent steps to be effective
- **② Aeration**: Pull cylindrical soil cores → allows air, water, and nutrients to penetrate
- **③ Overseeding**: Sow Country Green blend seed at 3–4 lbs / 1,000 sq ft
- **Best timing**: August–September (fall) — spring overseeding exposes seedlings to immediate summer stress
- **Home Depot Rental**: Aerator ~$100/day, Dethatcher ~$100/day

**Narration Script**:
> These three steps must be done in the right order. First, dethatch to remove dead organic material from the surface. Then use an aerator to punch holes in the soil so air and water can reach the roots. Finally, spread seed. The ideal window is August through September. Done in fall, roots establish over winter and you'll see a noticeably healthier lawn the following spring. You can rent both machines from Home Depot for around $100 a day each.

### Slide 20 `[TABLE]`
**Visual**: 🟩 REMOTION — Table slide layout (6 problems × 3 columns). With many entries, a table format works better than SVG.
**Title**: Problem Diagnosis and Treatment
**Subtitle**: Know the signs, know the solution
**Badge**: Problem Solving

**Table**:
| Problem | Symptoms | Treatment |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Grubs | Lawn lifts like carpet, C-shaped white larvae | Nematodes or insecticide |
| Chinch Bugs | Yellow-brown patches on sunny side | Insecticidal Soap |
| Raccoons / Moles | Dug-up lawn, tunnel signs | Eliminate grubs and they'll leave |
| Red Thread | Pink-red thread-like fungal strands | Fungicide + nitrogen fertilizer |
| Moss | Green moss in shaded or overwatered areas | Moss killer then dethatching |
| POA Weed | Spreading bright green patches | Hand-pull (herbicides ineffective) |

**Narration Script**:
> Here are the most common problems in Tehaleh and how to address them. When grubs eat the roots, turf peels up like a carpet. Treat with beneficial nematodes or an insecticide, then overseed to repair the damage. If raccoons or moles are digging up your yard, grubs are usually the cause — eliminate the grubs and the animals will move on. Red Thread spread across Tehaleh in spring 2026; a fungicide paired with nitrogen fertilizer is effective. Moss is the most common Tehaleh problem — preventing it with a low final mow and aeration before winter is always better than treating it afterward.

In summer, increase watering frequency and raise your mowing height to protect the roots. Fall is the most important renovation season of the year — do your dethatching, aeration, and overseeding in sequence between August and September. Then in November, just before winter, apply your winterizer fertilizer and do your final mow at 2 inches. And make sure to winterize your sprinkler system before the first freeze.
