## Summary

Spring in the Pacific Northwest (PNW) is the season when grass grows most actively. The year's first mow is not just a routine trim — it is the critical first step that determines the health of your lawn for the entire season. This guide covers the optimal mowing approach, taking into account the region's climate characteristics and the physiological rhythms of your grass.

---

## 1. Best Time to Mow (Timing)

When you mow affects both the stress on your grass and the efficiency of your equipment.

*   **Recommended (Late Morning / Late Afternoon)**:
    *   **10 AM – 12 PM**: The best time is right after morning dew has fully dried. Dry grass results in clean cuts and prevents equipment clogging.
    *   **4 PM – 6 PM**: Avoids the hottest part of the day, minimizing moisture loss through the cut surface.
*   **Not Recommended**:
    *   **Early Morning**: Wet grass promotes disease and can damage equipment.
    *   **Midday**: High temperatures cause rapid moisture evaporation through the cut blades, which can scorch the grass.

---

## 2. Watering Strategy

*   **Pre-Mowing**: **Never water before mowing.** The lawn must be completely dry for a clean cut.
*   **Post-Mowing**: Lightly watering right after mowing helps heal the cut ends. However, if rain is forecast for the next day, it is more efficient to let nature do the work.

---

## 3. Core Principles

### 3.1 The 1/3 Rule
This is the most important principle. **Never cut more than 1/3 of the total grass height in a single mow.**
*   Cutting too short (Scalping) reduces the grass's photosynthesis capacity, exposes the soil surface, and creates an environment where weeds thrive.
*   For the first mow of the season, it is safer to set the mower height a bit higher than usual (approximately 2.5–3 inches).

### 3.2 Equipment and Blade Check
*   **Sharp Blades**: Dull blades do not cut grass — they tear it. Torn blade tips turn white and become vulnerable to disease.
*   **Equipment Check**: Before the first run of the season, check fuel, tire pressure, and oil.

---

## 4. Effective Mowing Techniques

*   **Change Patterns**: Alternate the mowing direction (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) each time. Mowing in the same direction repeatedly compacts the soil and causes grass to lean to one side.
*   **Mulching**: If the clippings are small, leave them on the lawn instead of collecting them. As they decompose, they act as a natural nitrogen fertilizer and replenish soil nutrients.
