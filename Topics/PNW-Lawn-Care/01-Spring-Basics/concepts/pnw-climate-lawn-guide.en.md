## PNW Climate Characteristics

The Pacific Northwest has a unique rhythm for lawn care management.

### Annual Climate Pattern

| Season | Period | Monthly Rainfall | Temp | Grass Condition | Sprinkler Needed |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Winter | Nov–Feb | 100–150 mm | 2–8°C | Growth stalled | OFF |
| Early Spring | Mar–Apr | 80–100 mm | 8–14°C | **Peak growth starts** | Begin in May |
| Late Spring | May | 50–70 mm | 14–18°C | Vigorous growth | Starting to need |
| Early Summer | Jun | 30–50 mm | 17–22°C | Growth slowing | 2-3x/week |
| Midsummer | Jul–Aug | 10–20 mm | 23–30°C | **Stress/dormancy** | Daily or every other day |
| Early Fall | Sep | 20–40 mm | 18–23°C | Recovery begins | 1-2x/week |
| Fall | Oct | 60–90 mm | 10–15°C | **Second growth season** | Reduce |

### Tehaleh Specifics
- East Pierce County (near Bonney Lake) — slightly less rainfall than west side of Puget Sound
- Dry summers may last longer → higher dependence on sprinklers
- First frost: average mid-November, last frost: mid-March

## Cool-Season Grass Types

All lawn grasses suited for PNW Zone 8b are **Cool-season** varieties.
Optimal growth temperature: **10–20°C** (spring and fall)
Heat stress begins: **26°C and above** (PNW summers)

### PNW Standard Three Varieties

#### 1. Tall Fescue — Most Recommended
- Coarse, deep green blades
- Deep roots → **Best** drought tolerance (advantageous for PNW summers)
- Shade tolerance: Moderate
- Water requirements: Low to moderate
- Best for new lawns or repair (Overseeding)

#### 2. Perennial Ryegrass
- Fast germination (5–7 days) — great for quickly repairing bare spots
- Soft, shiny blades
- Drought tolerance: Low → needs more water in summer
- Commonly included in seed mixes

#### 3. Kentucky Bluegrass
- Beautiful deep blue-green color, soft texture
- Self-repairing (rhizomes) → naturally fills bare spots
- Water requirements: **High** → requires significant sprinkler use in PNW summers
- Shade tolerance: Low

### Seed Mix Recommendation
Most PNW lawn seed products blend all three varieties:
- Tall Fescue 60% + Perennial Ryegrass 30% + Kentucky Bluegrass 10%
- Look for "Pacific Northwest Mix" or "Northwest Blend" on the label

## Spring's Three Key Tasks

### 1. Dethatching

**Thatch**: The layer of organic matter (dried grass and roots) that builds up between the grass blades and soil

**Check if Needed**:
1. Push your finger into the base of the grass
2. If it feels spongy like a sponge → excess thatch (over 1.25 cm)
3. If firm or only slightly cushioned → normal

**How to Do It**:
- Tool: Dethatching Rake (manual) or electric Dethatcher (Home Depot rental ~$50/day)
- Mow short → Dethatch → Rake up removed thatch → Water
- Timing: Late March–early April (spring), or fall

**Note**: Thatch under 0.5 cm is actually beneficial (protects soil moisture)

---

### 2. Aerating

**Purpose**: Punch holes in compacted soil to improve roots' ability to absorb water, air, and nutrients

**Core Aeration**: Extracts 1 cm diameter soil cores spaced 2–3 cm apart
- Extracted cores can be left on the lawn to decompose naturally

**When Is It Needed?**:
- Standing on the lawn and it doesn't give much (soil compaction)
- Water pools on the surface after rain instead of absorbing
- New development areas (soil compacted during construction) — **Likely applies to Tehaleh**

**Tool**: Core Aerator rental (Home Depot ~$70/day)
**Timing**: Spring (March–April) or fall (September–October), once per year

---

### 3. Overseeding

**Purpose**: Seed bare or thinning areas of the lawn

**Best Timing**:
- Spring: Late March–April (soil temp above 10°C)
- Fall: September–early October (more recommended — long growth period after germination)

**How to Do It**:
1. Mow short (5 cm → 2.5 cm)
2. Aerate or Dethatch first
3. Spread seed (recommended rate: 20–40 g/m²)
4. Lightly rake to mix seed into surface
5. Water (keep soil moist until germination — shallow watering 2x daily)
6. Germination period: 7–14 days (depends on soil temperature)

**Important**: Do not use Pre-emergent (germination inhibitor) before Overseeding — it will also block lawn seed germination!

## Fertilizer Basics

### N-P-K Notation
Every fertilizer bag shows three numbers: **N-P-K**

| Component | Role | Spring Priority |
|-----------|------|----------------|
| N (Nitrogen) | Green leaf growth, color | ⭐⭐⭐ High |
| P (Phosphorus) | Root development | Moderate (at seeding) |
| K (Potassium) | Overall health, cold hardiness | More important in fall |

### Seasonal Fertilizer Recommendations

| Season | Recommended N-P-K | Example Product |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Early Spring (Mar–Apr) | 30-0-4 or 24-0-11 | Scotts Turf Builder Spring |
| Late Spring (May–Jun) | 32-0-6 | (Supports continued growth) |
| Summer (Jul–Aug) | Not recommended or minimal | Can worsen heat stress |
| Fall (Sep–Oct) | 24-0-11 or 0-0-20 | Scotts WinterGuard |

### Slow-Release vs. Quick-Release
- **Slow-release**: Releases gradually over 8–12 weeks → no burn, long-lasting → **Recommended**
- **Quick-release**: Immediate effect; can burn grass if over-applied or in heavy rain

### Application Timing
- 1–2 days before rain forecast is ideal (natural absorption)
- Morning dew or water immediately after applying
- No fertilizer in summer heat (burn risk)

## Weed Prevention (Pre-emergent)

### Pre-emergent Herbicide
Inhibits weed seed **germination** (cannot remove weeds already growing)

**Application Timing**: Before soil temperature reaches 10°C (50°F) → **Early to mid-March (now is the right time!)**
- For Tehaleh: Week 1–3 of March is the optimal window
- How to confirm: Soil thermometer or when Forsythia begins blooming

**Precautions**:
- **60–90 days** after applying pre-emergent, Overseeding is not possible
- If planning to Overseed, decide about pre-emergent first
- Overseeding and Pre-emergent cannot be done simultaneously — choose one
