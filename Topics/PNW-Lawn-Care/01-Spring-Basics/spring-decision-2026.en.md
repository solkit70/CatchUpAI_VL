## 🤔 Why Does This Decision Matter?

Pre-emergent and Overseeding **cannot be done at the same time**.

| Product | Role | Conflict Reason |
|---------|------|----------------|
| Pre-emergent | Blocks seed germination | Does not just block weed seeds |
| Overseeding | Spreads lawn seeds | Pre-emergent blocks lawn seeds too |

→ **Only one can be chosen** (at the same time)

## ⚖️ Option Comparison

### Option A: Pre-emergent Now (Recommended)

**When**: Mid-March (right now!)
**Target**: Both front and back yard

**Pros**:
- Completely blocks spring weeds (Crabgrass, Annual Bluegrass, etc.)
- Current lawn density is not terrible — blocking weeds alone is sufficient
- PNW March = Golden timing for pre-emergent (before soil reaches 10°C)

**Cons**:
- Thin areas in front yard cannot be repaired this spring → **must wait until fall**

### Option B: Overseed Now (Not Recommended)

**When**: If done now, late March–early April

**Pros**:
- Quick repair of thin areas in front yard

**Cons**:
- Cannot block weeds → weed competition in spring (weeds grow faster than lawn seeds)
- PNW spring is not ideal for Overseeding (fall is much better)
- New seedlings competing with spring weeds = uncertain outcome

---

## ✅ Final Decision: **Option A — Pre-emergent First**

### Rationale

1. **Timing**: Now is the golden window for pre-emergent (early to mid-March)
2. **Current Lawn Condition**: Front yard at C+ — Overseeding is not urgent
3. **PNW Season**: Spring Overseeding is not recommended (fall is optimal)
4. **New Development Characteristics**: Tehaleh new development = many weed seeds in soil → pre-emergent is even more important

### Decision Summary

| Timing | Task |
|--------|------|
| **Mid-March (Now)** | Pre-emergent application (front and back yard) |
| Early April | Spring fertilizer Slow-release N (can combine with pre-emergent) |
| June–July | Summer fertilizer (drought preparation) |
| **Early September** | Overseeding (thin areas in front yard) + Aerating |
| October | Fall fertilizer (winter preparation) |
