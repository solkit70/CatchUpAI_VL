## Records from people who went ahead

Finally, let's show you some actual records. **Not only what went well, but what didn't too.** Hear only good stories and you collapse at the first failure.

The numbers here all come from records that actually exist.

## The good stories

### 22 outputs in 9.5 hours

A record left while learning one new tool.

| | |
|---|---|
| Total learning time | **about 9.5 hours** (the plan was 7–10, so within range) |
| Actual working days | 5 days |
| Files made | **22** |

*— Clearly-BRD-PRD case record*

What it would've been like learning the same thing another way is in that record too. Watch a few videos and you feel "I get it," then forget a week later; read docs and the concepts are clear but **nothing stays in your hands.**

> ⚠️ **To be exact.** This case also mentions two intro videos, but **those videos are not included in the 9.5 hours.** The video production was separate follow-on work that took **about 8 more hours.** Saying "made the videos too in 9.5 hours" would be an exaggeration.

### It keeps getting faster

The table we saw earlier, again.

| The same task | Time taken |
|---|---|
| First | about 4.5 hours |
| Second | about 4 hours |
| Next, estimated | 2–3 hours |

*— VibeLearn AI Topic final retrospective (2026-02-27)*

**The first time takes longest.** That's good news. It means being slow now doesn't mean staying slow.

### It continues even after a four-day break

> Because there was a WorkLog, it was easy to pick back up on 7/23 where I stopped on 7/19.
>
> — Seattle Tech Week 2026 Topic final retrospective

**You can take breaks in between.** Even if you can't do it daily, as long as the record is there, you can come back.

## The stories that didn't go well

### Time estimates are often off

**They tend to be generous.** Finishing faster than expected was common (around 64–82% of the estimate).

But there are **cases off in the other direction.** Making things in several languages, or deploying what was made, wasn't well captured in the plan and took long. One retrospective has this reflection.

> The expected video length and M4 time were estimated much too low. … because deployment work wasn't sufficiently reflected in the Roadmap.
>
> — Tehaleh Community Video Topic final retrospective

**A plan is just a plan.** Being wrong isn't failure; you set it better next time.

### The tool-use itself is the barrier

This is the most honest point, and **the reason this guide exists.**

> Today's progress had the AI (Claude Code) do most of the actual research and document writing. If a civic-group worker used this method "alone," there **may be a barrier of having to first learn how to use AI agent tools** — going forward, supporting material that guides "how to plan together with AI" would help adoption.
>
> — FedWay Liberation Day Topic final retrospective (2026-07-26)

**The very guide you're reading is that "supporting material."** What was noted as needed was actually made.

### Cost can run out faster than expected

Doing a lot of document-making work, there's a record of **being interrupted three times by hitting the limit.** After raising the limit four times, the decision was made to switch to a cheaper way.

**Hitting the limit isn't a malfunction.** But it's good to be mentally prepared. Record what you did today and take a break.

### The time the AI shortened a form

Once the AI **shortened a 652-line form to 176 lines.** It kept only 27%.

> Original template: 652 lines → the file the AI made: 176 lines (only 27% kept)
>
> — VibeLearn AI Topic maintenance record (2026-03-21)

Because of this, the rule **"copy the form whole and don't shorten it arbitrarily"** was made. It's now nailed down in several places, so it's hard for it to happen again.

**Your job is one thing.** If a made file has `{ }` braces or odd symbols left in it, say this.

```
There are unfilled parts left in this file. Please remake it.
```

### The retrospective gets dropped first

Honestly, in the actual records too, **when things got busy the retrospective got dropped first.** Not many Topics left even a full final retrospective.

So **don't try to do it perfectly.** Leaving even a 5-minute daily retrospective is enough.

## So how should you read this?

Think of the numbers this way.

**Take them as encouragement, not as a promise.** 22 in 9.5 hours is a real record, but that person was already used to the tool. A first-timer takes longer. **That's normal.**

And these records' self-assessment scores tend to be generous. **Use them only for motivation.**

## Finally

You've come this far. To sum up:

**You have the tools.** VS Code, Claude, Claude Code, VibeLearn AI.

**You know the method too.** Set a goal and tell it before starting, make it plan first, leave records, ask when you don't know.

**One thing remains.** Actually starting something.

It doesn't have to be grand. Like what you made earlier, **one thing from your own hand, however small,** is enough. That one becomes material for the next, and as records pile up it keeps getting faster.

**The first time takes longest. And you've already passed that first time.**
