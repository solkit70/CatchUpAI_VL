## What this module decided

**Opened with "the AI has no yesterday."** Persuade about the need for records morally ("records are a good habit") and no one does it. We give the **functional reason** — without them the AI has no way to know yesterday, and you end up explaining from scratch each time.

**We stated "you don't have to write it yourself."** The six WorkLog items look daunting. That the AI writes it, and that what you actually do is one line — **"I got this far today, please record it"** — is placed at the end of the document.

**Encouragement in real numbers, reality in real numbers too.** 9.5 hours/22, 4.5h→4h→2–3h, the four-day gap — all measured values looked up from vault retrospectives. The not-good stories likewise come from actual records (time-estimate misses, the barrier self-critique, the credit limit, the form shortening, the dropped retrospective).

**We used the Clearly numbers exactly.** "9.5 hours → 22" is right, but **the two videos are a separate ~8 hours.** Blurring that would be an exaggeration, so it's marked in a ⚠️ box.

**We connected the "barrier" self-critique to why this guide exists.** The FedWay retrospective notes "supporting material would help adoption," and **this guide is that supporting material.** It's the passage that tells the reader "the document you're reading was made because it was judged necessary."

**We wrote to distrust the self-assessment scores.** Vault retrospectives' Self-Assessment tends to run 4.7–5.0. Citing that as evidence would be dishonest, so we say to "use it only for motivation."

**Closed with "the first time takes longest."** And followed with "and you've already passed that first time," confirming the reader who finished chapters 3–4.
