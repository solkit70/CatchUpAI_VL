## Facilitator Session Manual

A manual for a session where you sit with a participant and take them **from setting up a vibe coding environment to first success.** Written so you can prepare the next session from this document alone.

**Session length**: 2 hours (1:1) / 3 hours (2–4 person group)
**Participant output**: a working environment + one result file
**Participant document**: [Vibe Coding First Steps — Participant Guidebook](01-participant-guidebook.en.md)

## Before the session — must-check

### ⚠️ Ask this first (when scheduling)

**"What computer do you use? Windows, Mac, or Chromebook?"**

| Answer | Action |
|---|---|
| Windows / Mac | Can proceed |
| **Chromebook** | **Not possible with this method.** Have them prepare a different computer or adjust the schedule |
| Only iPad/tablet | Not possible. A laptop/desktop is needed |
| Company/school computer | Install permission may be blocked. **Recommend a personal computer** |

> **Discover a Chromebook on session day and the whole session is lost.** Always check in advance. With students, the Chromebook ratio is high, so be especially careful.

### Pre-session checklist

- [ ] Confirm the computer used (table above)
- [ ] Confirm they can install with **administrator privileges**
- [ ] Confirm internet connection (the AI uses the internet with every answer)
- [ ] **Prepare a payment method** — a paid Claude subscription is needed. A card that works for overseas payments
- [ ] **If a minor, a guardian present or payment agreed in advance**
- [ ] Send the participant guidebook link in advance (reading through chapter 2 speeds the session)

### Facilitator supplies

- [ ] The participant guidebook (printed or on screen)
- [ ] This manual
- [ ] Screen-share setup (for online sessions)
- [ ] The same environment on your own computer (for demo)

## Session order

### 0. Opening talk (10 min)

**Goal**: ease tension, give a picture of what happens today.

- First hear **what the participant wants to make.** This becomes the first Topic subject later
- Confirm once more, in words, "you don't have to know how to code"
- The teacher's story, briefly (30 sec)
- Announce today's time and **that there's a cost** in advance

> 💡 **If you can't hear what they want to do here, they'll flounder in chapter 4.** If vague, detour with "what do you spend a lot of time on lately?"

### 1. Why we do this (15 min) — participant guidebook ch 2

**Goal**: know "what and why" before starting the install.

Hit only the essentials. Don't try to explain everything.

- Vibe coding = describe it and the AI makes it. **Knowing what to make is still the human's job**
- **It's not only coding** — apply it to what they said in step 0
- Different from web chat — **it stays as files**
- The highway analogy — the on-ramp is what we cross today

### 2. Install (50–60 min) — participant guidebook ch 3

**Goal**: a working environment. **This stretch is the body of the session.**

| Order | Content | Time | Common sticking points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-1 | Install VS Code | 15 min | Mac "unidentified developer" / admin rights |
| 2-2 | Sign up for Claude + pay | 20 min | **Card declined**, confirmation email in spam |
| 2-3 | Install/log in to Claude Code | 10 min | Browser won't open, subscription not reflected |
| 2-4 | Make and open a folder | 10 min | Can't find folder location |
| 2-5 | Make `hello.md` to verify | 5 min | — |

**How to run it**

- **Have the participant do it themselves.** Do it for them and they can't do it alone next time. Guide by voice while watching their screen
- Confirm "you'll see this screen when it works" together at each step's end
- When stuck, open the [when-you-get-stuck document](../03-Setup-Environment/04-troubleshooting.en.md) **together** with the participant. They learn how to use the document too
- At the payment step, **step away briefly.** Don't hover while they enter card details

> ⚠️ **Don't skip 2-5 (hello.md verification).** This is where the participant first feels "the AI works on my computer." The best-reaction moment in the whole session.

### 3. VibeLearn AI + first Topic (25 min) — participant guidebook ch 4

**Goal**: the one-sentence-start experience + the plan.

- Download ZIP → open the folder (10 min)
- Start the first Topic with **the thing you heard in step 0** (10 min)
- When the approval gate appears, point out **"this means the AI won't run off on its own"** (5 min)

> 💡 **Never mention contributor-only setup (hook·pip·API key).** If they're curious, point to Appendix D and move on.

### 4. First success (15 min) — participant guidebook 4-3

**Goal**: one visible result.

- Have them pick the one of three branches **that draws them**
- When the result comes, **have them deliberately fix it once** — "ask it to change the color." This is the moment they learn physically that "asking to fix it" is the normal way to use it

### 5. How to keep going (15 min) — participant guidebook ch 5·6

**Goal**: able to go alone after today.

Don't explain everything. **Leave only three.**

1. Next time, open the `VibeLearn-AI` folder and say **"Please start today's learning"**
2. When finishing the day, **"I got this far today, please record it"**
3. When stuck, **don't wrestle alone for long — reach out**

### 6. Closing (5 min)

- Look at what was made today together
- Have them decide just one thing to try next
- Introduce Catch Up AI (https://catchupai.net/)

## Frequently asked questions

**"Is it OK if I don't know any coding?"**
It's fine. You won't write a single line of code today. Knowing what to make matters more, and you already have that.

**"Does this keep costing money?"**
Monthly. You can cancel any time, and start with the lowest tier. (Don't say the amount — **confirm it together on screen**; prices change.)

**"Can the AI see all my files?"**
Only the folder you open. And it usually asks first before editing a file.

**"What if I break something by clicking wrong?"**
You can undo. Tell the AI "please undo what you just changed."

**"My English isn't good."**
Speak in your language. Only the screen buttons are in English, and I'll show you those.

**"How long does it take to learn this?"**
Two hours today gets you started. After that, learning by doing is faster.

## Mistakes facilitators easily make

**Doing it for them.** Grab the keyboard because you're short on time and the participant can't do it alone next time. **However slow, have the participant do it themselves.**

**Trying to explain everything.** Hit only the essentials in chapters 2 and 5·6. Install is the body. Explain concepts at length and there's no time left to actually install.

**Skipping first success.** When behind, cut chapter 5 (how to keep going), **never first success.** Without it, the participant won't reopen.

**Using hard words.** Folder/open/set-up instead of repository/directory/run/install. See the [plain-language glossary](../01-Guide-Foundation/02-glossary.en.md).

**Saying "error."** Say "a common thing that happens" instead. To someone unused to computers, "error" reads as "I did something wrong."

## When short on time — the order to cut

When behind, cut in this order.

1. **Finish chapter 5 (how to keep going) with just the three** — point them to the guidebook
2. **Compress chapter 1 (why we do this) to 5 minutes**
3. Trim the step-0 opening talk

**Never cut**: 2-5 (hello.md verification), 4 (first success).

## After the session

- [ ] Record the participant's **sticking points** → add to the [when-you-get-stuck document](../03-Setup-Environment/04-troubleshooting.en.md)
- [ ] Note where the guidebook's **explanation didn't land** → reflect in the next revision
- [ ] Follow up with the participant (a week later, "have you tried it since?")

> As session records pile up, this manual gets more accurate. **This guide itself is a case of getting better through records.**
