## What is vibe coding

Before we start installing, let's spend five minutes on what we're actually trying to do. **Starting out knowing what you're doing and why makes it far less likely you'll give up when you hit a snag.**

## In one sentence

**Vibe coding is a way of working where you describe what you want in plain language, and the AI handles the details.**

You decide what to make and whether it's right. The AI actually makes it.

## It used to be like this

Making something on a computer took two things.

**First, knowing what to make.** What's needed, why, and who will use it. The person who has worked in a field for years knows this best.

**Second, the skill to make it.** Learning a programming language, memorizing syntax, hunting down and fixing errors. This takes years to learn.

The problem was that these two rarely landed in the same person. **The person who best knew what to make usually didn't know how to make it.**

## Now it's like this

The second one has shrunk dramatically. Now you can just say this:

```
I want to make a health journal for the elderly folks in my neighborhood.
The text should be large, with only a few buttons.
```

And the AI actually makes it. **The first part — knowing what to make — is still the human's job.** And that's the part you already have.

> If you've worked in one field your whole life, you're actually already prepared.

## So does the AI do everything?

No. This part is best said honestly.

**The AI is not a magic wand.** If you say "just make something good," you're likely to get something plausible but unusable. Keep bolting on features without a plan and you end up in a state that's hard to fix later.

> "First set a plan and build from step 1" is a better approach than "finish it all at once."

And one more thing. Just because the AI makes it doesn't mean you can know nothing. You do need the habit of asking **"why is this set up this way?"** or **"what happens if I change this here?"** You don't need to become a developer, but you do need enough to **steer the AI.**

That steering part is exactly what this guide is trying to teach.

## But why is starting so hard?

Here lies the reason this guide exists.

The stretch AI made fast is **just one stretch.** Picture a 100-mile, 4-lane highway where **only 5 miles have been widened to 10 lanes.** On that 5-mile stretch (the making) you get astonishingly fast.

But **the on-ramp into that wide stretch is still narrow.** Knowing what to install, where to start, what to ask — knowing that itself is the barrier.

**Even with knowledge and experience, just coming up with "what do I need to know and how do I ask" is hard.**

This guide was made to walk that narrow on-ramp together. You only have to cross it once.

## Someone actually did this

There's a teacher in their 60s in Utah, USA. Not someone especially used to computers.

**Helping them set up their computer was all it took.** Within a few days, they made — themselves — an app that records their life story, organizes it into text, and turns it into a book.

Not because of special talent. **The experience they'd built up in one field was already enough raw material.** All that was missing was the on-ramp.

## To sum up

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Knowing what to make | Human | **Still human** |
| Actually making it | Human (years of study) | **AI** |
| Judging whether it's right | Human | **Still human** |
| What it takes to start | Learning to program | **One environment setup** ← this guide |

What you already have carries over unchanged. The only new thing to learn is **how to work with AI.**
