## AI is a coworker, not a tool

The same tool gives very different results. The fork is one thing: **do you treat the AI like a search box, or like a person you work with.**

## Two ways

| Same situation | Used like a search box | Handed off like a coworker |
|---|---|---|
| Preparing a talk | "What is Liberation Day?" | "I'm preparing a 5-minute talk. The audience is middle schoolers — what should I decide first?" |
| Files are scattered | "How do I organize well?" | "Look at the files in this folder and split them into decided and still-undecided." |
| Something to make | "How do I make an app?" | "I want to make an app like this. Set a plan first." |
| Stuck | "What is this error?" | "I'm stuck here. Tell me in order what I should check." |
| Work is done | (closes the window) | "Organize what I did today and record it." |

The left side gives answers too. But they're **answers used once and gone.**

The right side is different. You give it your situation, hand off the judgment, and the result stays as a file. **You can pick up where you left off next time.**

## Why this difference happens

A search box gives **one answer per question.** That's all.

A coworker is different. **They know the context.** What you're trying to do, how far you've come, where you're stuck. So they cover things you didn't ask about and suggest what to do next.

Treating AI like a coworker means **giving the AI context.** And that context piles up not in a conversation but **in files.**

## Like handing work to a good coworker

Imagine handing work to someone new. However capable they are, this rarely goes well:

> "Just handle it well."

But say this and it changes:

> "I want to organize the materials for this event. The goal is for the next person in charge to understand it right away. The current materials are in this folder, and the deadline is next week. Organize a plan first and tell me."

**What, why, how far.** Telling it these three is the core. Don't overthink it — just explain like you would to the coworker next to you.

## And — make it plan first

This is a principle many people repeat. That's a sign of how important it is.

> **Don't start by asking the AI to build the whole thing; ask for a plan first.**

Say "make it" and the AI starts making right away. But if that's not what you wanted, by then it's already far along. Undoing it is more of a hassle.

**Say "set a plan first"** and you can see the direction and correct it. Fixing a plan takes 5 minutes; fixing something already made takes far longer.

The tool we'll install has a feature built exactly for this. It's called **Plan mode** — a mode where the AI plans **what it will do and how, without editing files right away.** We'll walk through it again later.

## Why this way keeps getting better

This may be the most important part of this guide.

Working with AI like a coworker means **records pile up.** What you did today, where you got stuck, what you decided — it all stays as files.

Those records aren't just memos. **They become material the AI reads next time.** As records accumulate, the AI comes to know who you are, what method you like, and how far you've come.

> **Records make AI stronger. Your records make an AI that's yours.**

Ask the AI using only information anyone can find, and you get answers like everyone else's. But give the AI **your own records** and you get answers no one else could.

## Three things to remember now

Before we start installing, just these three are enough.

**One — don't ask like a search; give context and hand it off.** What, why, how far.

**Two — before asking it to make, make it plan first.** Seeing the direction first is much faster.

**Three — leave records.** That's the material for next time's AI.

You'll pick up the rest as you go. **You don't have to understand it all now.**
