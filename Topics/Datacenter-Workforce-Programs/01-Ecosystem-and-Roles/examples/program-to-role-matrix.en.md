## Four takeaways

### 1. Concentrated in construction-phase trades — 4 of 6

①③④⑤ all target construction trades. **Most of these programs are not a path to
Big Tech employment** — the employer during construction is a partner construction
company (see [employment-structure.en.md](../concepts/employment-structure.en.md),
Misconception 1).

Hypotheses for why:
- Construction labor demand is overwhelmingly large (the "vast majority" of jobs
  from a new data center announcement are temporary construction jobs)
- Each project needs a large workforce quickly, so an ongoing training pipeline is required
- Building on existing union apprenticeship infrastructure (NABTU, etc.) lets programs scale fast

### 2. Construction × Technician really is empty

0 of 6. This matches the hypothesis formed while drawing the map —
**there are no servers while the building is under construction.**
It looks structural, not a research gap.

⚠️ But six is only the baseline set. M2 gathers more programs and re-checks whether
this cell stays empty.

### 3. ⑤ Amazon TWD straddles two cells — possibly not a single program

"Fiber + electrical/power infrastructure" (construction) and "data center operations"
(operations) are mixed into one entry. This shape usually means
**several separate sub-tracks under one umbrella brand.**

→ M2 needs to break ⑤ into its sub-tracks. Treating it as one program mixes up
requirements, duration, and application channels.

### 4. The cell most relevant to me personally is the bottom right — provisional

Operations × Technician (②⑥) connects to existing IT/AI experience, and its entry
role, Data Center Technician, has the lowest barrier — **HS diploma + on-the-job training.**

⚠️ **This is an observation, not a conclusion.** Per roadmap rule 3, both tracks are
investigated with equal depth through M7. There's also a bridge from a trades
apprenticeship into Critical Facilities Engineer (see
[role-map.en.md](../concepts/role-map.en.md), takeaway 2) — leaning one way now
would miss that path.
