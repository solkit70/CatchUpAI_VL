## Summary

If M2 showed "what each of the 6 programs actually is," M3 works out **"where each
of those programs sits within the credential system."**

Under the same phrase "training program," four entirely different things were
mixed together — **registered apprenticeship · pre-apprenticeship · certificate ·
degree.** Which form a program takes changes everything: **whether you're paid
during training, what's left when it's over, and where you actually apply.**

## Key findings

### 1. Only registered apprenticeship pays during training

The DOL's own phrase is **"Apprenticeships are jobs!"** You're an employee, not a
student, paid from day one, with pay rising as skill increases.

The other three (pre-apprenticeship, certificate, degree) all mean **no income
while learning.**

> **Even if the cost is $0, a year with no income is, in real terms, a year of
> living expenses.** For a career change, this one factor can outweigh every
> other condition.

### 2. Reclassifying the 6 programs by credential type

| Program | Credential type | Income during training |
|---|---|---|
| ① Meta AWA | Certificate | **Yes** (free + daily stipend) |
| ② MS Datacenter Academy | Certificate / Degree | None |
| ③ MS NABTU | Pre-apprenticeship + supplemental education | None |
| ④ Google.org / etA | **Registered Apprenticeship** | **Yes** |
| ⑤-a AWS WBLP | **Registration status unconfirmed** — employment relationship exists, paid | **Yes** |
| ⑥ Amazon Apprenticeship | **Registered Apprenticeship** (DOL-certified) | **Yes** |

### 3. ⑤-a's classification is ambiguous — an item for M5

WBLP is 12 months paid, followed by placement into a full-time AWS role.
**It resembles a registered apprenticeship in having an employment relationship
and pay, but DOL registration status isn't stated on the official page.**

| If registered | If not registered |
|---|---|
| Nationally recognized credential | An internal AWS work history |
| Recognized when changing employers | Counts only as experience |

M2 judged WBLP the strongest candidate — **this item changes how strong that
judgment actually is.**

### 4. The two tracks have completely different entry gates

| | Track A — trades | Track B — technician |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | **Selection by the local union JATC** — competitive, with waitlists | **Applying to a job posting** |
| Duration | 8,000 hours (roughly 4–5 years) | 12 months (WBLP) |
| Final employer | A union construction company (project-based) | A data center operator (ongoing) |

M1's finding that **"Construction × Technician is a structural gap"** is confirmed
again at the pathway level. **The people who build and the people who run the
facility enter through different doors from the start.**

### 5. The standards documents already have the answers

Items that were blank in the comparison table — **starting apprentice wage
percentage, physical requirements, selection scoring** — aren't blank from a lack
of research. They're **set separately, per program, in the WSATC Program
Standards PDF.** There's no single nationwide value.

→ Opening the Puget Sound Electrical JATC standards (WSATC-0134) in M5 fills these in.
