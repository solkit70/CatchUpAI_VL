## Why this distinction matters

Breaking down the 6 programs in M2 revealed that **very different things were
hiding under the same phrase "training program."** ⑥ Amazon Apprenticeship is a
DOL-registered apprenticeship, ② MS Datacenter Academy is a college certificate
program, and ③ NABTU's expansion is short free training. **Which type a program
is determines whether you're paid during training, what's left when it's over,
and what happens if you drop out partway through.**

## Comparing the 4 types

| | **① Registered Apprenticeship** | **② Pre-apprenticeship** | **③ Certificate** | **④ Degree** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Certifying body | **US Department of Labor (DOL) or a state apprenticeship agency** | None (varies by program) | Educational institution / industry association | Educational institution (regionally accredited) |
| Employment relationship | **Yes — you are hired** | No | No | No |
| Income during training | **Yes (paid, escalating wage)** | Usually unpaid | None | None |
| Cost | Free or low-cost | Usually free | Tuition | Tuition |
| Duration | 1–5 years (by trade) | Weeks to months | 6 months–1 year | 2 years (associate)+ |
| Outcome | **Nationally recognized credential** + journeyman status | Apprenticeship eligibility/priority | Certificate/credential | Degree |
| If you leave partway | Work history / partial hours count | Little carries over | Only what was completed | Credits earned |

## ① Registered Apprenticeship

The US Department of Labor's own definition:

> "an industry-driven, high-quality career pathway where employers can develop and
> prepare their future workforce, and individuals can obtain **paid work
> experience** with a mentor, receive **progressive wage increases**, classroom
> instruction, and a **portable, nationally-recognized credential**."

Five core elements are stated explicitly — **Industry Led · Paid Job · Structured
On-the-Job Learning/Mentorship · Supplemental Education · Credentials**.

**"Apprenticeships are jobs!"** — the DOL's own phrase. You are an **employee**,
not a student.

### The decisive difference of this type — no unemployed period

The other three types all mean **no income while you learn.** Only registered
apprenticeship pays during training, with wages stepping up as skill increases.

For someone changing careers while still needing to make a living, this one factor
can outweigh everything else. **Even a $0-cost program means, in real terms, a
year of living expenses if there's no income for a year.**

### Registered vs. unregistered

Only programs **"industry-vetted and approved and validated"** by the DOL or a
state apprenticeship agency count as registered apprenticeships. Unregistered
training hasn't gone through that vetting, so

- the credential **may not carry nationally** (a problem when changing jobs or moving)
- there's no guaranteed wage-escalation structure
- there's no recourse if the program is discontinued

**Veterans can receive GI Bill benefits on top of wages** (explicitly stated by the DOL).

## ② Pre-apprenticeship

A preparation phase taken **before** applying to an apprenticeship. Usually
unpaid, but short and free.

It serves two purposes — **preparing for apprenticeship entrance exams/interviews**,
and **securing priority selection into an apprenticeship program.** ③ NABTU's
TradesFutures is this type.

**It is not, by itself, a job qualification.** If it doesn't lead into an
apprenticeship, little is left over.

## ③ Certificate

Issued by an educational institution or industry association. ② MS Datacenter
Academy is this type — Big Bend CC's **Systems Administration – Datacenter
Specialization** 1-year certificate.

It costs tuition (which a scholarship may cover) and there's no employment
relationship. **Completion doesn't guarantee a job** — this is why M2 found ②'s
"post-completion employer" to be "not stated."

## ④ Degree

Associate (2-year) or higher. ② Datacenter Academy also has a 2-year associate path.

## 🔑 Stackable credentials

A structure where credentials build on each other. Pre-apprenticeship →
registered apprenticeship → journeyman, for example — **each stage feeds into
the next, and something is retained even if you stop partway.**

The opposite also exists — non-stacking structures. A short-course certificate
often isn't a prerequisite for the next stage.

## Classifying the 6 programs by credential type

Reading the M2 anatomy table through this lens:

| Program | Credential type | Income during training |
|---|---|---|
| ① Meta AWA | ③ Certificate (NCCER + America's Workforce Certificate) | **Yes** (free + daily stipend) |
| ② MS Datacenter Academy | ③ Certificate or ④ Degree | None |
| ③ MS NABTU | ② Pre-apprenticeship (TradesFutures) + supplemental education | None |
| ④ Google.org / etA | ① **Registered Apprenticeship** (IBEW apprenticeship) | **Yes** |
| **⑤-a AWS WBLP** | Registration status unconfirmed — **employment relationship exists, paid** | **Yes** |
| ⑥ Amazon Apprenticeship | ① **Registered Apprenticeship** (DOL-certification stated explicitly) | **Yes** |

### ⑤-a's classification is ambiguous

WBLP is **12 months of paid training followed by placement into a full-time AWS
data center role.** It resembles a registered apprenticeship in having an
employment relationship and pay, but **DOL registration status is not stated on
the official page.**

| | If registered | If not registered |
|---|---|---|
| Credential | Nationally recognized | An internal AWS work history |
| When changing jobs | Recognized at other employers | Counts only as experience |

→ **An item to confirm in M5.** Looking up "AWS" in the apprenticeship.gov
program search or the WA L&I registration list would settle it.
