# Two-Minute FDE Explanation

## Version 1: For a Non-Technical Audience

A Forward Deployed Engineer is an engineer who works very close to the customer, not just from inside the product team. Their job is to understand how the customer actually works, identify where software or AI can help, build or adapt the solution, and make sure it is adopted in real operations.

Think of it this way: a normal product team may build a powerful tool, but the customer still has messy data, unclear processes, internal approvals, security rules, and users who need the tool to fit their day-to-day workflow. The FDE sits in that gap. They translate the customer's real problem into a technical implementation and bring lessons back to the product team.

In AI companies, this is especially important because a model demo is easy, but a production AI workflow is hard. The FDE helps move from "the model can answer a question" to "this AI system safely improves a real business process."

## Version 2: For a Software Engineer

An FDE is a customer-facing engineer who owns the path from ambiguous customer problem to working deployment. The role can include discovery, scoping, prototype implementation, integration with customer systems, eval design, rollout planning, and product feedback.

Compared with a regular SWE role, the FDE spends more time in the customer's context and deals with ambiguity earlier. Compared with a solutions engineer, the FDE is usually more responsible for building and deploying. Compared with a consultant, the FDE must turn recommendations into technical artifacts.

For AI FDE roles, the technical surface often includes APIs, backend services, data connectors, RAG, agent workflows, evals, observability, security boundaries, latency and cost trade-offs, and customer enablement.

## Version 3: For a Career Conversation

FDE is a strong fit for people who like both engineering and customer problem solving. It is not ideal for someone who wants to only write isolated product code, and it is not ideal for someone who wants to only advise without implementation responsibility.

The best FDE candidates can show evidence of three things:

1. They can build or deeply understand technical systems.
2. They can work with customers or stakeholders in ambiguous environments.
3. They can connect technical decisions to business adoption.

For juniors, that evidence usually comes from portfolio projects. For senior IT professionals, it can come from delivery, operations, customer, or stakeholder experience translated into FDE language. For non-IT domain experts, the most realistic path is often through FDE-adjacent roles first.

## 30-Second Summary

An FDE is a customer-facing engineer who turns real customer workflows into working software or AI systems. The role matters because demos do not equal adoption. In the AI era, FDEs help customers move from model experiments to secure, evaluated, production-ready workflows.
