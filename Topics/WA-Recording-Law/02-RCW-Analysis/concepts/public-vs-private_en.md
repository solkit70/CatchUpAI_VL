# Public Places vs. Private Spaces — Standards for When Recording Is Permitted

## Core Judgment Standard

> Is there a **reasonable expectation of privacy**?
>
> - Yes → private space → consent required
> - No → public place → recording may be allowed (but the content of the conversation still requires separate judgment)

## Classification by Location

| Location | Privacy expectation | Video recording | Audio recording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public streets and parks | None | ✅ | ✅ (private conversations of others are ❌) |
| Public rallies and protests | None | ✅ | ✅ |
| Public seminars and lectures | Low | ✅ (check policy) | ⚠️ Check with organizer |
| Paid or non-public events | Medium | ⚠️ Organizer consent | ❌ Organizer consent required |
| Cafes and restaurants | Low | ⚠️ | ⚠️ Others' conversations ❌ |
| Company conference rooms | High | ❌ Consent required | ❌ Consent required |
| Online meetings | High | ❌ Consent required | ❌ Consent required |
| Private residences | Very high | ❌ | ❌ |

## Difference Between Video Recording and Audio Recording

**Video recording**: Broadly protected by the First Amendment. Generally legal in public places.

**Audio recording**: Subject to a separate law, RCW 9.73. Even in public places, intentionally capturing a specific person's private conversation can be risky.

> Even within the same seminar video recording:
> - Recording the presenter's lecture screen → ✅ generally OK
> - Capturing private conversations of attendees nearby with a microphone → ❌ risky
