# Legal/Illegal Judgments by Scenario

> Based on Washington State RCW 9.73. ✅ Legal / ❌ Illegal / ⚠️ Conditional

---

## Scenario 1: Ambient recording in an office with Limitless AI Recorder

**Judgment**: ❌ **Illegal** (if others have not consented)

**Reasons**:
- Every conversation with coworkers is recorded without consent
- Even if the purpose is PKM, the **act of recording itself** is illegal
- Personal-use purpose does not affect legality

**Safe alternatives**:
- Keep it on only when you are alone
- Before a conversation, say: "I am recording with Limitless right now"

---

## Scenario 2: Recording a public conference speaker's lecture on video without checking with the organizer

**Judgment**: ⚠️ **Conditional** (organizer policy must be checked)

**Reasons**:
- Recording a speaker's public remarks at a public event is generally allowed
- If the organizer announced "no recording," recording may breach the event terms
- Even public presentation videos may still be copyrighted by the speaker

**Recommended procedure**: Before the event starts, ask the organizer: "May I record this for personal learning purposes?"

---

## Scenario 3: Using Zoom's built-in recording feature as the meeting host

**Judgment**: ✅ **Legal**

**Reasons**:
- Zoom's built-in recording feature automatically notifies all participants
- This structurally satisfies constructive consent
- This is the safest and recommended method

---

## Scenario 4: Secretly recording a coworker's unfair remarks with a smartphone

**Judgment**: ❌ **Illegal** (in ordinary circumstances)

**Reasons**:
- In Washington State, even a conversation you participate in cannot be recorded without the other party's consent
- The intent to preserve evidence does not affect legality
- Illegal recordings are not admissible as evidence in court

**Exception**: If the other party makes clear threats, intimidation, or extortionate statements, exception ② may apply.

---

## Scenario 5: Recording a lecture after getting the professor's permission by email

**Judgment**: ✅ **Legal**

**Reasons**:
- The professor's explicit consent makes the recording fully legal
- Email also leaves a record as proof

**Tip**: Ask by email at the beginning of the semester and state that it will be used "only for personal review."

---

## Scenario 6: Recording a seminar without permission and uploading it to YouTube

**Judgment**: ❌ **Doubly illegal**

**Reasons**:
1. **Recording law violation**: no consent from organizer, speaker, or attendees (RCW 9.73)
2. **Copyright infringement**: the speaker owns copyright in the presentation content

---

## Additional Scenarios

### Screen-recording an online class

**Judgment**: ⚠️ **Mostly prohibited**

- Often prohibited by school policy
- Separate copyright issue exists because the professor's lecture is a copyrighted work
- Whether it is allowed depends on school policy and professor consent

### Another table's conversation is captured in my recording at a cafe

**Judgment**: ⚠️ **Gray area**

- If it is unintentionally captured as background sound, it is generally not a problem
- If you intentionally aim a microphone at a specific person's conversation, it is risky
