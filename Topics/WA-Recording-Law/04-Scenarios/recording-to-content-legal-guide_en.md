# Complete Legal Guide for Recording/Filming → Content Creation and Publication

> Based on Washington State law + federal law
> **⚠️ Not legal advice — consult an attorney for actual legal judgments**
> Created: 2026-05-31

---

## Core Structure: The Law Applies Separately at Two Stages

```text
[Stage 1] Audio/video recording act
          ↓
[Stage 2A] Publishing the recording as-is as content
[Stage 2B] Processing it into new content and publishing it
```

**Important**: Even if Stage 1 is legal, Stage 2 may be illegal. If Stage 1 is illegal, Stage 2 automatically becomes a larger problem.

Different laws apply at each stage.

---

## Stage 1: The Recording/Filming Act

### Applicable Laws

| Law | Jurisdiction | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal Wiretap Act (18 U.S.C. § 2511) | Federal | One-party consent |
| RCW 9.73.030 | Washington State | **All-party consent** (stricter) |

### Judgment Principle

In Washington State, **all-party consent** applies. If you record without the consent of all conversation participants, the act is already illegal at this stage. Regardless of later stages, criminal and civil liability for the Stage 1 violation remains.

> If Stage 1 is illegal:
> - Criminal: gross misdemeanor (up to 364 days + $5,000)
> - Civil: $100/day up to $1,000 + attorney fees
> - The recording is inadmissible as evidence in court

## Stage 2A: Publishing the Recorded Content As-Is

### Additional Laws Applied Separately from Stage 1

#### ① Copyright Law (Federal, 17 U.S.C.)

The recorded content may contain **the other party's copyright**.

| Content type | Copyright protection | Impact when published |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lectures, presentations, speeches | ✅ Speaker's work | Unauthorized publication = copyright infringement |
| Musical performances | ✅ Performer's/composer's work | Unauthorized publication = copyright infringement |
| Films/video screenings | ✅ Creator's work | Unauthorized publication = copyright infringement |
| Everyday conversations | ⚠️ Protected if creative | Gray area |
| Simple facts/information | ❌ No copyright | The content itself may be publishable |

**Core point**: Recording someone's lecture, presentation, or performance and uploading it as-is to YouTube can be copyright infringement. Even if the event was public and free, unauthorized distribution of the copyrighted work is a separate issue.

**Fair Use (17 U.S.C. § 107)**

Even if copyright exists, fair use may be recognized after considering four factors:

```text
① Purpose and character of the use
   - Commercial purpose → unfavorable
   - Nonprofit, education, criticism, news, research → favorable
   - Transformative use → favorable

② Nature of the original work
   - Creative work such as music or fiction → stronger protection
   - Factual information such as news or data → weaker protection

③ Amount used
   - Entire work → unfavorable
   - Excerpt → favorable
   - But using the "heart" of the work may be unfavorable even if short

④ Market effect
   - Harms the market for the original work → unfavorable
   - No market effect → favorable
```

> ⚠️ Fair use is judged afterward by a court.
> You can be sued even if you believed it would be fair use.

#### ② Privacy Torts (Federal/Common Law)

Even if Stage 1 was legal because recording consent was obtained, publication may still trigger privacy torts:

| Tort type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public disclosure of private facts | Publishing private information that a reasonable person would object to publicizing | Publishing a recording of a medical consultation |
| False light | Publishing true facts in a misleading context | Editing only part of a conversation to distort intent |
| Defamation | Publishing false facts that damage reputation | Distorting statements and publishing them |

**Important**: Even if a private conversation was recorded with consent, publishing that conversation without the other party's consent can lead to a privacy lawsuit.

#### ③ Right of Publicity (Washington State RCW 63.60)

Washington State protects a person's name, voice, signature, photograph, and likeness as **property rights**.

```text
RCW 63.60.010: A person's name, voice, signature, photograph, and likeness
are property rights and may be freely transferred or licensed.
They continue permanently after death.
```

**Application**: Publishing a recording containing someone's voice or face for a **commercial purpose** may infringe their right of publicity.

**Exceptions (RCW 63.60.070)**: Use without consent may be allowed for:
- News reporting, public issues, historical or educational purposes
- Satire and parody
- Fictional creative works, if not misleading
- Incidental or insignificant use

## Stage 2B: Processing Recorded Data into New Content and Publishing It

### Spectrum of Processing

```text
[Original as-is] ──────────────────── [Fully transformed]
       ↑                                      ↑
Higher copyright infringement risk      Higher fair use possibility
Higher privacy infringement risk        Lower publicity rights risk
```

### Legal Judgment by Processing Type

#### Type 1: Editing, summaries, highlights

Making short clips or highlights from the original recording.

| Item | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Copyright | ⚠️ Uses the original work. If transformation is low, fair use is difficult |
| Privacy | ⚠️ If editing changes context, false light risk |
| Publicity rights | ⚠️ Consent needed for commercial use |

**Example**: Editing a seminar presentation into a 5-minute YouTube video → high copyright infringement risk.

#### Type 2: Commentary/Criticism

Quoting recorded content while adding your own opinion or analysis.

| Item | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Copyright | ✅ Higher fair use possibility because of criticism/commentary purpose |
| Privacy | ⚠️ Still problematic if private conversations are published |
| Publicity rights | ⚠️ Critical commentary may be allowed; commercial appropriation is not |

**Example**: Quoting part of a lecture and adding criticism in a YouTube video → fair use may be possible.

#### Type 3: Transcript Use (AI summary/organization)

Converting a recording into a transcript and organizing or summarizing it.

| Item | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Copyright | ⚠️ The transcript itself is a reproduction of the original work. Summary/organization may be transformative |
| Privacy | ⚠️ Publishing the content may risk disclosure of private facts |
| Personal use | ✅ If not published and used only for personal PKM, risk is lower |

**PKM use**: Consent-based recording → transcript → personal Obsidian storage → **legal**
Public seminar recording → transcript → blog publication → **possible copyright infringement**

#### Type 4: AI-generated derivative content

Using recorded data as AI input to create new content.

| Scenario | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| AI summary from lecture transcript → blog publication | ⚠️ Derivative based on original work. Fair use review needed |
| Creating an AI voice clone from someone's voice recording | ❌ Publicity rights infringement (RCW 63.60) + consent required |
| AI writes a completely new article based on seminar ideas | ✅ Ideas are not copyrighted. Using information rather than expression is generally OK |
| Meeting recording → AI meeting minutes → internal team sharing | ✅ Legal if participants consented. Low copyright risk for internal use |

> **Important**: Ideas, facts, and information are not protected by copyright.
> What is protected is the **specific expression** of those ideas.
> → Writing my own article based on what I learned from a lecture is legal.
> → Taking a lecture transcript as-is and publishing it under my name is illegal.

#### Type 5: News reporting/documentary

Using recorded content for reporting or documentary purposes in the public interest.

| Item | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Copyright | ✅ Higher fair use possibility due to news reporting purpose |
| Privacy | ✅ Public interest may outweigh privacy |
| Publicity rights | ✅ RCW 63.60.070 news/public issue exception may apply |

**Condition**: There must be actual public interest, and the content must not be false.

## Practical Scenario Matrix for Creators

| Scenario | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Overall judgment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public lecture recording → personal review | ✅ (organizer consent) | ✅ (private) | **✅ Legal** |
| Public lecture recording → YouTube as-is | ✅ (organizer consent) | ❌ copyright infringement | **❌ Illegal** |
| Lecture summary + criticism → YouTube | ✅ (organizer consent) | ⚠️ Fair use review | **⚠️ Conditional** |
| Use lecture ideas → write my own article | ✅ | ✅ ideas are not copyrighted | **✅ Legal** |
| Meeting recording → AI summary → internal team | ✅ (participant consent) | ✅ internal use | **✅ Legal** |

## Safety Guide for Creators

### Safest Method

```text
① Before recording: "This conversation/lecture will be recorded and used for [purpose]"
   → clarify both consent and purpose

② Before publishing: separately ask "May I publish this content?"
   → consent to recording ≠ consent to publication

③ When quoting lectures/presentations: use short clips of 5-10 seconds and add criticism/commentary
   → increases fair use possibility

④ Never do the following:
   - Upload an entire lecture without permission
   - Create an AI clone from a voice obtained without consent
   - Publish private conversations (separate publication consent is needed even if recording was consented to)
```

### Pre-Publication Checklist

```text
□ Did all participants consent at the recording stage?
□ Did they separately consent to publication?
□ Am I avoiding direct use of copyrighted material as-is?
□ Do fair use factors apply, such as criticism/commentary/education/transformative use?
□ Am I using another person's face or voice for commercial purposes?
□ Does the edit distort the context of the statement?
```
