# Federal Law vs. Washington State Law — Comparing Recording Consent Standards

## Key Difference

| Item | Federal Law (Federal Wiretap Act) | Washington State Law (RCW 9.73) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consent standard | **One-party consent** | **All-party consent** |
| Conversation I participate in | ✅ I can record if I consent | ❌ The other party's consent is also required |
| Recording without the other party knowing | ✅ Legal under federal law | ❌ Illegal in Washington State |
| Criminal penalty level | Federal felony (up to 5 years in prison) | Gross misdemeanor (up to 364 days in jail) |
| Scope of application | U.S.-wide minimum standard | Washington residents and conversations within Washington |

## State Law Controls

```text
Federal law OK + state law OK → legal
Federal law OK + state law no → illegal in Washington State (state law controls)
```

> 38 U.S. states + D.C. = one-party consent
> About 12 states, including Washington, California, and Florida = all-party consent

## Practical Meaning

If you live in Washington State, you should not think only in terms of federal law.
"I am part of the conversation, so it should be okay to record" → **This is wrong in Washington State.**

## Two Ways to Obtain Consent

1. **Explicit consent**: "May I record this?" → the other person says "Yes" → consent is established
2. **Constructive consent**: You say "This conversation is being recorded," and if the other person continues the conversation, consent is established. This is the most commonly used practical method.
