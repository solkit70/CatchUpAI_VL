# Round 1 (2025) — The Four Selected Teams

The City of Bellevue's first Challenge in 2025 received **23 applications and selected 4**. The co-chairs of Bellevue's Innovation Forum sub-teams reviewed them against the posted criteria.[^partners] All four ran **AI pilots** with the city and Bellevue College.

> This page separates **facts** (sourced) from **interpretation** (marked 🔎). Team scores and reasons for selection were not published, so "why they were chosen" is our reading against the public criteria.

## At a glance

| Team | Based in | What it does | Pilot partner | What the pilot tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Certivo** | Seattle | AI platform for **vendor compliance** in procurement and cybersecurity | City | Whether it streamlines compliance monitoring and speeds decisions |
| **Legislaide** | Denver, CO | AI analysis of **municipal codes, legislative history and state statutes**; plain-English search of agendas, minutes, ordinances, resolutions | City | Whether it surfaces insights and simplifies legal and policy drafting |
| **Juganu** | Israel | **Smart lighting** that illuminates curbside activity and captures real-time usage patterns | City and Bellevue College | Support for transportation and public-safety initiatives |
| **FindIt** | Built by a local **high school student** (15 at the time) | AI **lost-and-found** app | Bellevue College | Usability and effectiveness for students, staff and visitors; potential for broader use |

Sources: City of Bellevue news and participants page[^partners][^participants]

## Team notes

### Certivo
- **Fact**: A Seattle company with an AI platform giving near-real-time visibility into vendor compliance across procurement and cybersecurity. The company announced its selection.[^participants][^certivo]
- 🔎 **Reading**: A **workflow-level** fix that reduces manual checks for a city managing many vendors. The product already existed.

### Legislaide
- **Fact**: A Denver company whose AI analyzes municipal codes, legislative history and state statutes, letting staff search agendas, minutes, ordinances, resolutions and reports in plain English.[^participants]
- 🔎 **Reading**: Proof that **a company outside Washington was selected**. A document-search AI for daily staff research — similar in spirit to round 2's "public records search with AI" use case.

### Juganu
- **Fact**: An Israeli company. Smart lighting that lights curbside activity while capturing real-time usage patterns, supporting city and Bellevue College transportation and public-safety work.[^participants] In the U.S. it installed its first full smart-city lighting in Peachtree Corners, Georgia (report based on company statements).[^juganu]
- 🔎 **Reading**: A **non-U.S. company** with a pilot spanning **two institutions** — an example of round 2's "jurisdictional benefit" criterion.

### FindIt
- **Fact**: An AI lost-and-found app built by a local high school freshman (15 at the time). Staff photograph found items; AI writes a description (e.g., "blue water bottle with red sticker"); students type a description and the AI finds matches. It was already on iOS.[^geekwire] The pilot tested usability and broader potential on the Bellevue College campus.[^participants]
- 🔎 **Reading**: Round 1 **explicitly encouraged youth** (parents sign for under-18s).[^round1call] FindIt combined a small, clear problem, a working app and real users on campus. **Round 2 requires applicants to be 18+**, so the same solo-student path is no longer open.

## What the four had in common

| Pattern | Basis | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **All AI** | City news: "All four are piloting AI projects"[^partners] | Fact |
| **Working products already existed** | Three companies with existing products; FindIt was on iOS[^geekwire] | Fact |
| **Tied to specific staff or user workflows** | Compliance checks, legal research, curb management, lost items | 🔎 Reading |
| **Small, clear pilot scope** | One department or one campus, measuring usability and effect | 🔎 Reading |
| **Local and international teams** | Seattle, Denver, Israel, a local student | Fact (round 2 also accepts out-of-state teams — Oct 26 office hours; non-U.S. unconfirmed) |
| **Two pilots included the college** | Juganu, FindIt | Fact |

## How it turned out

The city reports that milestones (Dec 2025, Jan 2026, Mar 2026) were completed and **pilots concluded in May 2026**.[^accomplishments] **Team-level results were not published.** The city summarized that the pilots demonstrated civic engagement examples, generated practical insights, validated emerging technologies and identified service improvements; one design partner said collaborating with others on a real problem was the most meaningful part.[^program] A council update described the Challenge as contracting with **four businesses** to pilot solutions such as AI compliance dashboards, smart lighting and mobile item lookup.[^council]

**Follow-up results heard at the Oct 26 office hours**: **two of the four teams are doing follow-up work with the City of Bellevue or Bellevue College**, and **one is selling to other governments** using what it learned in Bellevue (the timing was not right for Bellevue, but it was a great product). Results depended on how each pilot went; the teams were not named. The hardware (streetlight) pilot took longer than software because of permits; Bellevue College is installing more of the lights and cameras on campus, and the FindIt app is in use at Bellevue College. → [What we confirmed](../../04-Application-Guide/concepts/office-hours-findings.en.md)

> Even so, **a purchase or follow-on contract after the pilot is not guaranteed** (call FAQ).
