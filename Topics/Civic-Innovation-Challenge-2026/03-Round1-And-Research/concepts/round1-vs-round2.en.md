# Round 1 (2025) vs Round 2 (2026–27) — What Changed

| Item | Round 1 (2025, Bellevue) | Round 2 (2026–27, Exchange Northwest) |
|---|---|---|
| Host | City of Bellevue (Innovation Forum)[^r1] | City of Bellevue, Bellevue College, King County IT, WaTech[^r2o] |
| Selection | 3–5 planned → **4 selected** (23 applicants)[^r1][^acc] | **4–6** planned[^r2o] |
| Stipend | **Up to $15,000**[^r1] | **$15,000–$45,000**, most at the lower end[^r2o] |
| Who can apply | Individuals, teams, **students**, start-ups, businesses; youth encouraged (parents sign for under-18s); city employees excluded[^r1] | Individuals, teams, start-ups, businesses; **18+**; authority to sign a contract[^r2o] |
| Topics | Solutions supporting Bellevue's strategic targets, Council priorities and department challenges (no use-case list)[^r1] | **8 use cases** by agency plus each agency's priorities[^r2u] |
| Rubric | 4 — community engagement and inclusion, alignment with city priorities, feasibility/sustainability/ability to deliver, innovative approaches[^r1] | **9** — adds project overview, product maturity, jurisdictional benefit, evaluation and impact, overall quality[^r2s] |
| Application | 10 narrative questions (175 words) + 2-minute pitch video[^r1] | **16 questions** (175 words) — adds timeline, implementation, cost proposal, AI[^r2s] |
| Finalist paperwork | Signed agreement | W-9/TIN, certificate of insurance, supplier-portal registration[^r2s] |
| Office hours | 2 sessions (virtual and in person)[^r1] | 3 (Oct 14 in person, Oct 26 in person, Nov 4 virtual 10-minute slots)[^r2o] |
| Timeline | Plan: open Jun 4, due Jul 8, winners Aug 8, Sep 4 – Jan 30, 2026[^r1] → Actual: selection in October, pilots ended May 2026[^acc] | Open Oct 14, **due Nov 13**, start Jan 19, 2027, end Jul 3[^r2o] |
| Sponsors | Amazon, Bellevue Downtown Association, Microsoft, Salesforce, Slalom, T-Mobile[^r1] | Not listed |

## What it means (our reading)

- **Bigger program.** Four agencies instead of one, and the stipend ceiling tripled. Use cases now cover transit, parks and state services, not just city administration.
- **More focus on deployability.** The rubric grew from 4 to 9 criteria, with product maturity, jurisdictional benefit and evaluation as separate items, and the form now asks for an implementation plan and a cost proposal. **Deployable solutions** matter more than ideas.
- **Solo student applications are harder.** Round 1 encouraged youth and selected a high-school team; round 2 requires applicants to be 18+.
- **New paperwork hurdles.** Finalists need a tax ID and insurance, and King County use cases require vendor registration.
- **Dates can slip.** Round 1's selection came about two months late and the pilots ended about four months late.
