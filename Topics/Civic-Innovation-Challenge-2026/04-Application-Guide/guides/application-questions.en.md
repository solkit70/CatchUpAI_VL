# How to Answer the 16 Application Questions

The application is a web form, with **175 words per question**.[^submission] In the table, "What reviewers look for" comes from the official rubric; "How to write it" and "Common mistakes" are **our guidance** based on the rubric and round-1 selections. The question-to-rubric links are in the [rubric map](../../02-Use-Cases-And-Criteria/concepts/rubric-map.en.md).

> 175 words is short. Aim for **one message per question**, with one number and one example.

| # | Question | What reviewers look for | How to write it | Common mistakes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Contact, website, secondary contact | — | Always add a secondary contact (they reach out if documents are missing) | No website — prepare at least a product page |
| 4 | **Project overview** | ① overview · ⑤ maturity · ⑥ jurisdictional benefit | "The agency's work problem → our solution → what we'll test → **which agency and why**." Add **your current stage** (prototype, pilot users, in market, number of users) in one line | Describing the technology without the agency's problem |
| 5 | **Project timeline** | ④ feasibility | Fit "install → test → measure" in **five months**, assuming 1–2 hrs/week from the design partner and 2–3 hrs/month from a mentor | Promising too much in five months |
| 6 | Alignment with priorities | ③ alignment | **Quote your chosen use case** and connect your solution to it | Generic statements unrelated to the agency |
| 7 | Innovative approaches | ⑦ innovation | Show **how** the technology improves service (time, accuracy, access) | Listing buzzwords |
| 8 | **Community engagement and inclusion** | ② community | Who benefits, **equity and underserved groups**, collaboration with **students, nonprofits or community groups** | Vague "good for everyone" |
| 9 | Team composition | ④ ability to deliver | 2–4 key people, their roles and relevant experience | Résumés without roles |
| 10 | **Milestones and timelines** | ④ · ⑤ maturity · ⑧ impact | Deliverables over 6–8 months, **when deployable**, hypotheses to test; restate maturity evidence | Milestones without measures |
| 11 | **Implementation** | ④ · ⑥ jurisdictional benefit | Compatibility, training, **pilot-to-full-deployment phases**, data and integration, **use by other agencies** | No plan beyond the pilot |
| 12 | Partnerships and collaborations | ② community | Partners and their roles; **openness to working with similar teams** | Names without roles |
| 13 | **Evaluation and impact** | ⑧ evaluation | **Metrics in numbers** (e.g., 30% less processing time, user satisfaction), how you'll measure, barriers the partnership can help with | "It will improve efficiency" without metrics |
| 14 | **Cost proposal** | ④ sustainability | Total pilot cost (with installation) and future one-time and ongoing costs; remember most stipends are at the **lower end** | Inflating to the stipend ceiling |
| 15 | AI practices | ⑦ innovation | If AI is involved, say yes — an **AI questionnaire** follows. Be ready to explain data handling and human review | Hiding AI; no data-protection explanation |
| 16 | **2-minute pitch video** | ⑨ overall quality | See the [pitch video guide](pitch-video.en.md). Check the link opens and include any password | Over two minutes; link permission errors |

## Advice from the Oct 26 office hours

- **Decide first what you want to prove with the government over six months.** It should be **a specific part**, not your whole plan. Applications that said "we'll do whatever you want" were hard to evaluate. → State this one sentence consistently in questions 4, 5 and 10.
- The sweet spot is where **what your business learns from working with government** meets **how you help the agency**. The speaker also said that if government is not your primary customer, think about whether it is worth your time.
- **Company size doesn't matter.** "You in the garage" or 20 people — describe where you are honestly. Startups and small businesses are weighted equally.
- **Already partnering with or being recommended by another organization is "not a deal killer."** Describe partners in question 12. Whether letters of support from community groups affect the score was not confirmed.
- The speaker said "I think 12 questions" but sounded unsure. **Prepare for the call's 16 questions at 175 words each.**

Source: Oct 26, 2026 in-person office hours, Tukwila → [What we confirmed](../concepts/office-hours-findings.en.md)

## Lessons from round-1 teams (our reading)

All four round-1 teams attached a **working AI product** to a **specific agency workflow** in a **small pilot** ([round-1 teams](../../03-Round1-And-Research/examples/round1-selected-teams.en.md)). To show this in your application:

- **Working product** → current stage and evidence in questions 4 and 10
- **Specific workflow** → connect the use-case wording and the agency's work in questions 4 and 6
- **Small, measurable pilot** → five-month scope and numeric metrics in questions 5 and 13
- **More than one agency, or a college partner** → questions 11 and 12 (two of the four round-1 pilots involved the college)

## Writing with AI

This methodology is also about directing AI well. Draft with AI, but **check every fact and number yourself**.

```text
Write question 4 (Project overview) of the Civic Innovation Challenge application in English, within 175 words.
- Chosen use case: (paste the use case name and wording)
- Our solution: (one or two sentences)
- Current stage: (prototype / N pilot users / in market)
- Agency we want to work with and why: (agency, reason)
Make the rubric items "project overview, product maturity, jurisdictional benefit" visible.
Do not invent any numbers or facts I didn't give you.
```
