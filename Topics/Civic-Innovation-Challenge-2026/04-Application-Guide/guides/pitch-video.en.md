# 2-Minute Pitch Video Guide

Question 16 asks for a **2-minute elevator pitch video link**, and the rubric's "overall quality" criterion names a **"high-quality elevator pitch video."**[^submission] It is the only deliverable named in the rubric. The structure and tips below are **our guidance** based on the rubric.

## A 2-minute structure (example)

| Time | Content | Rubric link |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **The agency's problem** — one number or scene from the use case (e.g., "40,000 customer comments a year") | ① overview · ③ alignment |
| 0:15–0:50 | **Solution demo** — screen recording of the real thing | ⑤ maturity · ⑦ innovation |
| 0:50–1:20 | **Pilot plan** — what you'll test in five months and **what you'll measure** | ④ feasibility · ⑧ impact |
| 1:20–1:40 | **Who benefits** — community and underserved groups; use by other agencies | ② community · ⑥ jurisdictions |
| 1:40–1:55 | **Team** — one line per person | ④ ability to deliver |
| 1:55–2:00 | One closing sentence | ⑨ quality |

## Tips

- **Stay under two minutes.** Write the script first and time it out loud (usually 130–150 words per minute).
- **Show a demo.** A real screen shows a working solution better than slides.
- **Leave one number in their memory** — the size of the problem or the expected effect.
- **Add captions.** Some reviewers watch without sound.
- **Clarity over polish.** A phone camera plus screen recording is enough (our reading).
- **Check link permissions.** Upload as YouTube "unlisted" or similar and open it on another device without logging in. Include any password in the form.

Inputs: (use-case wording, our solution, current stage, metrics, team)
