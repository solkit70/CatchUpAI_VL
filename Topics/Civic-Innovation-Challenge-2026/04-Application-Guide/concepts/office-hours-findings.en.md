## Other things we learned

**Application and review**

- The application comes as a **PDF** (to draft offline) and a **web form**. It covers your idea, **the specific part you want to prove with the government over six months** (not your whole plan), what your company looks like now ("you in the garage" is okay, so is 20 people), a website, and a **2-minute pitch video** (an iPhone video is fine).
- The speaker said "I think 12 questions," but sounded unsure. **Follow the call: 16 questions, up to 175 words each.**
- Review starts by ranking applications **high, medium, low**; finalists are invited for a conversation.
- **Startups and small businesses are weighted equally.** Round 1 got many innovative ideas from small businesses.
- **Employees of Bellevue, King County and Washington State cannot apply.** Otherwise it is "wide open."
- The deadline is **strict**; late applications were not accepted last year.

**After selection**

- **Each government writes its own six-month contract**, and they will look different. **Hardware was slower than software** because of permits (the round-1 streetlight pilot).
- The main value is **the government as "customer zero,"** not the money. The speaker said "if you're here for just the money, you're probably in the wrong room," and that if government is not your primary customer, think about whether it is worth your time.
- Mentoring: in round 1 one mentor met each of the four teams weekly for an hour if they wanted, and all four found it helpful.
- Annual event: about 350 people last year; planned again for **late May or early June in Bellevue** (date not set).
- Bellevue and King County each intend to award "a couple," and the state about one (the call says 4–6 overall).

**From the group discussion** (what participants want from the Exchange)

- The most common story: **creating multiple accounts for business licenses and permits in systems that don't connect** — directly related to the state's one-stop-shop use case.
- Others: help entering government contracting, accessibility in procurement and licensing (including for people with disabilities), a digital resource hub for seniors and people with limited tech access, more youth involvement, and community nonprofits struggling to find government grant and program information.
