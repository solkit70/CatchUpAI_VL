## City of Bellevue

Bellevue also welcomes proposals that support its strategic target areas and Council priorities. Questions: innovation@bellevuewa.gov.[^usecases]

### 1. Accelerating public records search and review with AI
- **Problem**: Staff must search many systems to find records for public records requests.
- **What they want**: AI and large language models that interpret a request **by meaning and context**, search authorized city repositories, and prioritize likely responsive records — even when wording differs. Staff still review responsiveness, privilege, exemptions and redaction.
- **Good fit for**: Teams building document search, RAG or records-management AI
- **Watch out**: Data security and human final review are central

### 2. Traffic camera video identify and capture automation
- **Problem**: Under the Public Records Act (RCW 42.56), the city must diligently search all systems, including traffic camera footage, by hand.
- **What they want**: Enter location, date, time and incident details (car make, model, color, action); the system scans traffic camera footage, finds the exact clip and downloads it. Clerk's Office staff validate it.
- **Good fit for**: Video search and computer vision teams
- **Watch out**: Access to video data and privacy handling

### 3. Digital twin for Wilburton and the Grand Connection
- **Problem**: A major redevelopment area that needs coordination across land use, transportation, parks, utilities and economic development.
- **What they want**: A virtual environment that models pedestrian activity, public space use, development scenarios, environmental impact and connectivity, so planners, developers and residents can compare designs.
- **Good fit for**: 3D, GIS, simulation and urban-planning visualization teams
- **Watch out**: The test goal is better public engagement

## King County (KCIT)

**To be considered for a KCIT design partnership, applicants must register in the King County E-Procurement Supplier Portal.**[^usecases]

### 4. AI-driven qualitative analysis of maintenance comments
- **Problem**: King County Metro receives **40,000+ customer comments a year** but mostly handles them one by one. Work orders for illegal dumping, unsanitary conditions and hazardous waste at stops have risen since 2022.
- **What they want**: Continuous AI analysis of maintenance comments to surface **hotspots, timing and severity** early, improve service for underreported areas and low-income riders, and expand to other departments if successful.
- **Good fit for**: Text analytics and voice-of-customer AI teams
- **Watch out**: Equity for underserved riders is an explicit goal

### 5. SMART-alerts: AI for accessible and accurate transit alerts
- **Problem**: Metro is the largest transit agency in the Northwest (about 289,000 weekday trips in June 2026, 132 routes, ~7,000 stops) and sends about **1,291 district alerts a month**. A small team writes them by hand from many systems.
- **What they want**: A secure generative AI parser that reads reroute documents, checks them against GIS and GTFS data and drafts alerts; **plain-language** rewriting, consistency checks, and later **multilingual alerts starting with Spanish**. Staff review before publishing.
- **Good fit for**: Document parsing, generative AI and transit data teams
- **Watch out**: Human-in-the-loop review at every stage

### 6. Harnessing AI to understand parks visitation
- **Problem**: Park and trail visits are counted by hand or estimated.
- **What they want**: Use anonymized mobile-location data to estimate visits for maintenance, capital planning, equity of access and project evaluation.
- **Good fit for**: Location-data analytics teams
- **Watch out**: Privacy protection is key

### 7. Community-driven ideas for Executive policy goals
- **What they want**: Innovative ideas for King County's goals: (1) **Breaking the Cycle** — homelessness, addiction, incarceration; (2) **Building More** — housing, childcare, transit, infrastructure; (3) **Be in Community** — a more visible, connected government; (4) **Better Government** — transparent, efficient, accountable.
- **Good fit for**: Teams with solutions for these goals; the widest open category
- **Watch out**: Be specific about which goal and which problem you solve

## Washington Technology Solutions (WaTech)

### 8. Start Your WA State Business: One-Stop-Shop
- **Problem**: New business owners research and apply to multiple agencies on their own.
- **What they want**: One online "front door." Owners answer a few questions (business type, industry, location, employees, subcontractors) and get a **personalized checklist of licenses, permits, registrations and certifications** with links to apply and status tracking. Existing owners manage their information in one place and get **renewal and requirement-change alerts**. It connects existing tools (BLS, SOS filing) rather than replacing them.
- **Good fit for**: Government-service UX, guided wizards and regulatory data teams
- **Watch out**: The experience of first-time and immigrant business owners can connect to the rubric's community and equity criteria
