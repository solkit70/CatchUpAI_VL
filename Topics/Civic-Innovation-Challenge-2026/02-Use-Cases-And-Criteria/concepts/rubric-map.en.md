# The 9 Rubric Criteria and the 16 Application Questions — Where to Earn Each Score

The application has **16 questions, 175 words each**, and reviewers score it on **9 rubric criteria**.[^submission] The criteria and questions are not paired one to one. This page maps **which question should show each criterion**, and it is the backbone of the question-by-question guide in Module 4.

## 1. The 16 questions

| # | Question | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Contact, website, secondary contact | — |
| 4 | Project overview | Problem, solution, what you hope to achieve, **which agency and why** |
| 5 | Project timeline | **What you will test** with a government partner and how, in **five months** (1–2 hrs/week with the design partner, 2–3 hrs/month with a mentor) |
| 6 | Alignment with priorities | Specific links to Exchange Northwest partner priorities |
| 7 | Innovative approaches | Modern practices and how they improve service delivery |
| 8 | Community engagement and inclusion | Broader community benefit, equity, underserved populations |
| 9 | Team composition | Key members, roles, short bios |
| 10 | Milestones and timelines | Deliverables over 6–8 months, **when ready for deployment**, what to test |
| 11 | Implementation | Compatibility, training and onboarding, **pilot-to-full-deployment phases**, data and integration |
| 12 | Partnerships and collaborations | Partners, openness to working with similar teams |
| 13 | Evaluation and impact | **Success metrics**, barriers the partnership can help address |
| 14 | Cost proposal | Total pilot cost, financial structure, future costs |
| 15 | AI practices | If AI is involved, an AI questionnaire follows |
| 16 | **2-minute elevator pitch video** | Link |

## 2. Rubric ↔ question map

| Rubric criterion | What reviewers look for | Main questions | Supporting |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① Project overview | Clear problem, well-defined solution, goals of the partnership | **4** | 16 |
| ② Community engagement and inclusion | Broad community benefit, **collaboration with students, nonprofits or community groups**, equity | **8** | 12 |
| ③ Alignment with priorities | Evidence of fit with agency goals and initiatives | **6** | 4 |
| ④ Feasibility, sustainability, ability to deliver | Realistic deployment plan, sustainable methods, **replicable elsewhere**, timeline fits milestones | **5 · 10 · 11** | 9 · 14 |
| ⑤ Product maturity | Concept → prototype → pilot with feedback → market use → adoption evidence | (no dedicated question) show in **4 · 10** | 11 · 16 |
| ⑥ Jurisdictional benefit | Who benefits, **number of jurisdictions** | **4 · 11** | 6 |
| ⑦ Innovative approaches | AI, analytics, 5G, digital twins, smart tech, creative processes | **7** | 15 |
| ⑧ Evaluation and impact | Meaningful metrics and KPIs, evaluation plan | **13** | 10 |
| ⑨ Overall quality | Clarity, polish, **high-quality pitch video**, value to the agencies | **16** | all |

**Things to notice**

- **⑤ Product maturity has no dedicated question.** State your stage and users yourself in questions 4 and 10 so reviewers can score it (our reading).
- **⑥ Jurisdictional benefit** also connects to stipend size: the call says stipends depend on "whether they are applicable to more than one government or education partner."[^overview]
- **② Community** asks directly about collaboration with students, nonprofits and community groups. Use it together with question 12.
- **⑨ The pitch video** is the only deliverable named in the rubric.

## 3. Compared with the first round (2025)

| | Round 1 (2025, Bellevue) | Round 2 (2026–27) |
|---|---|---|
| Rubric | 4 criteria: community engagement and inclusion, alignment with city priorities, feasibility/sustainability/ability to deliver, innovative approaches | **9 criteria** |
| New criteria | — | **Project overview, product maturity, jurisdictional benefit, evaluation and impact, overall quality (pitch video)** |
| Maturity | Asked inside feasibility ("mature enough to deploy?") | Its own criterion with five levels |
| Application | 10 narrative questions + pitch video | 16 questions — adds **timeline, implementation, cost proposal, AI** |
| Priorities | Bellevue strategic targets, Council priorities, department challenges | 8 use cases by agency + each agency's priorities |

Round 2 looks much more closely at **whether a solution can really be deployed** (implementation, cost, maturity, use across agencies). Where round 1 emphasized good ideas and community, round 2 asks more about practical, usable solutions (our reading).[^round1]
